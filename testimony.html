My testimony

A lot of my life has been about making complicated things easier for other people. I’m good at looking at a situation, working out what’s getting in the way and figuring out what we can do about it. Then I hit a point where I was burnt out, and being good at figuring things out wasn’t enough. I needed time to admit how tired I was and look honestly at what I was building my life on.

I think of Kuala Lumpur as the place where I had a spiritual awakening. I’m still finding the words for what happened there. What I can say is that I came to see that I needed to rebuild some of my foundations. For me, following Jesus has meant looking at the parts of my life I’m used to managing myself and asking what it would actually look like to trust him with them.

I remember listening to a church service online and hearing someone say, “You are a saint.” They went on to explain that they were talking about who you are. Cue me bursting into tears. I didn’t expect that sentence to get me. I think I’d spent so much time looking at what needed fixing that I had a hard time taking in what God was saying about who I already was in Christ.

That’s where grace has become real for me. I can be honest about where I’m struggling and still believe that Jesus loves me. I can ask for help. I can take responsibility for what’s mine without assuming that everything is mine to carry. I’m still learning how to do that in ordinary life.

My faith also makes me ask practical questions. If a child stops coming to church because participating is hard, could we call the family and ask what would help? Could we make something as simple as a visual guide so the child knows what to expect when they walk in? I love thinking about those details. Sometimes they’re the difference between a family wanting to come and feeling like they actually can.

I’m still rebuilding. There are days when I want to have the whole thing figured out, and there are days when I can see that God is doing something in the middle of it. I’m learning to trust Jesus there, and to pay attention to who might need a little help finding their place.
